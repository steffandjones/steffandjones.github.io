ES51 Engineering Design

All-Terrain Autonomous RC Vehicle · Harvard

Project Overview

ES51: Computer-Aided Machine Design is Harvard's foundational engineering design course that challenges students to apply principles of mechanical design, electronics, and programming to create functional robots. Our team designed and built an all-terrain RC vehicle capable of autonomous navigation and object manipulation.

The vehicle featured a robotic arm system designed to grab balls and cubes, raise them to different heights, and place them precisely onto scoring podiums. The competition format awarded points based on the number and accuracy of objects successfully placed, requiring both mechanical precision and strategic design.

Key Features

  • All-terrain drivetrain with enhanced traction and stability
  • Multi-degree-of-freedom robotic arm with gripping mechanism
  • Autonomous navigation and object detection system
  • Precise positioning system for accurate object placement
  • Integrated control system for coordinated movement
  • Optimized for speed and scoring efficiency

Technical Design

The project integrated mechanical engineering, electronics, and software to create a cohesive system. The chassis was designed for stability across varied terrain while maintaining maneuverability. The robotic arm incorporated servo motors for multi-axis movement, with a custom gripper mechanism capable of securely grasping objects of different shapes and sizes.

Mechanical Systems: The drivetrain featured independent motor control for differential steering, while the arm mechanism used linkages and gearing to achieve the required reach and lifting capacity. CAD modeling in SolidWorks ensured all components fit within size constraints and maintained proper weight distribution.

Control & Programming: Microcontroller-based control system coordinated drivetrain movement with arm positioning. Sensor integration enabled autonomous navigation and object detection, while custom algorithms optimized scoring strategy and movement efficiency.

This course provided hands-on experience with the complete engineering design process—from initial concept through prototyping, testing, iteration, and final competition.
