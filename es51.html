ES51 Engineering Design

All-Terrain Autonomous RC Vehicle · Harvard

Project Overview

ES51: Computer-Aided Machine Design is Harvard's foundational engineering design course that challenges students to apply principles of mechanical design, electronics, and programming to create functional robots. Our team designed and built an all-terrain RC vehicle capable of autonomous navigation and object manipulation.

The vehicle featured a robotic arm system designed to grab balls and cubes, raise them to different heights, and place them precisely onto scoring podiums. The competition format awarded points based on the number and accuracy of objects successfully placed, requiring both mechanical precision and strategic design.

Key Features

  • Fully custom-built chassis, arm, grabber, and wheels — nothing off the shelf
  • Dragon Skin silicone rubber wheels, cast in-house for maximum grip on varied terrain
  • Custom grabber designed and fabricated by the team for reliable object capture
  • Drive motors and gears salvaged and repurposed from an old power drill
  • Multi-degree-of-freedom robotic arm with precise positioning for scoring podiums
  • Optimized for speed and scoring efficiency in competition

Technical Design

Almost everything on Blue Mamba was made by the team from scratch. The chassis, arm, and grabber were all custom-designed in SolidWorks and fabricated in-house. The wheels were cast using Dragon Skin silicone rubber, chosen for its high grip and durability across the competition surface. The drive motors and gearbox were salvaged from an old power drill and repurposed into the drivetrain, keeping the build resourceful and low-cost without sacrificing performance.

Mechanical Systems: The drivetrain used the reclaimed drill motors with independent control for differential steering. The arm mechanism used custom linkages to achieve the required reach and lifting capacity, with a grabber designed specifically to handle both balls and cubes reliably. CAD modeling in SolidWorks ensured all components fit within size constraints and maintained proper weight distribution.

Control & Programming: A microcontroller-based system coordinated drivetrain movement with arm positioning. Custom algorithms optimized the scoring strategy and movement sequencing across the competition.

This course provided hands-on experience with the complete engineering design process, from initial concept through prototyping, testing, iteration, and final competition.
