BLUE
MAMBA

All-terrain autonomous RC vehicle with robotic arm — custom chassis, silicone-cast wheels, repurposed drill motors, and a multi-DOF arm for scoring competition objects.

ES51: Computer-Aided Machine Design is Harvard's foundational engineering design course, challenging students to apply mechanical design, electronics, and programming to create functional robots. Our team designed and built an all-terrain RC vehicle capable of autonomous navigation and object manipulation.

The vehicle featured a robotic arm designed to grab balls and cubes, raise them to different heights, and place them precisely onto scoring podiums. Competition points were awarded based on number and accuracy of objects placed — requiring both mechanical precision and strategic design.

CAD & DESIGN

Designed all custom components — chassis, arm, grabber, and wheel moulds — in SolidWorks, building parametric assemblies and engineering drawings for each part. The course emphasised designing for manufacture: running interference checks, specifying tolerances, and iterating geometry when prototypes revealed problems. CAD was a continuous loop throughout the project.

FABRICATION

ES51 functions as Harvard's introduction to the engineering workshop. CNC milling for precision metalwork, laser cutting for flat-pattern parts, and FDM 3D printing for complex geometry. Dragon Skin silicone rubber wheels were cast in-house using a two-part mould. Drive motors and gearbox came from a disassembled power drill, stripped and remounted in a custom chassis bracket for differential steering.

MECHANICAL

The chassis was designed around a low centre of gravity, with all mounting points laid out in SolidWorks before any material was cut. The arm used a multi-link mechanism to achieve the reach and lift height needed for scoring podiums at varying heights. The grabber was designed to close passively on contact with both balls and cubes without the operator switching grip modes mid-run.

SOFTWARE

A microcontroller served as central control, receiving RC receiver input and translating commands into coordinated motion across the drivetrain and arm. Custom code managed sequencing of scoring runs with timing tuned through repeated practice. The control architecture remained modifiable so scoring strategy could be adjusted between rounds.
