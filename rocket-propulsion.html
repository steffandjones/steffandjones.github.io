Harvard Rocket Propulsion

Ivy League's First Liquid Bi-Propellant Rocket

Project Overview

Member of the Harvard Rocket Propulsion Group (HRPG), the student-led team building the Mojave Sphinx: the Ivy League's first contemporary liquid bipropellant rocket. The group was founded with a clear mission: to design, build, and test a flight-capable liquid bipropellant engine and provide students with real industry-relevant aerospace engineering experience.

The Mojave Sphinx runs on nitrous oxide and an alcohol-based fuel, with a propulsion system built around a heatsink combustion chamber, scrintle injector, and a custom spark torch igniter. A key milestone during my involvement was achieving stable combustion in a successful hot fire of the spark torch igniter, a result that required iterative design, careful instrumentation, and field testing at Minuteman Airfield in Stow, Massachusetts.

Key Contributions

  • Achieved stable combustion in a successful hot fire of the spark torch igniter
  • Contributed to propulsion system fabrication and integration

  • Part of the team delivering the Ivy League's first liquid bipropellant rocket engine

Technical Work

Propulsion: Worked on the scrintle injector and heatsink combustion chamber designed for reliable ignition and stable combustion of nitrous oxide and alcohol fuel. The spark torch igniter — a critical sub-system — required precision in design and careful management of propellant flow, with multiple iterations based on data from previous test attempts.

Fluids System: Contributed to the propellant feed system, which uses servo-actuated ball valves, stainless steel and PTFE feedlines, and passive dual-acting vapor pressure pressurization to control the full propellant flow path from tanks to combustion chamber.

Testing: Supported test stand setup and instrumentation for static fire testing at Minuteman Airfield in Stow, Massachusetts. Data and control systems handled remote ignition commands and logged chamber pressure and valve state throughout each burn.
