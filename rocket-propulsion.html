ROCKET
PROPULSION

Harvard Rocket Propulsion Group — building the Ivy League's first liquid bi-propellant rocket. Achieved stable combustion in a successful hot fire of the spark torch igniter.

Member of the Harvard Rocket Propulsion Group (HRPG), the student-led team building the Mojave Sphinx: the Ivy League's first contemporary liquid bipropellant rocket. The group was founded to design, build, and test a flight-capable liquid bipropellant engine and give students real industry-relevant aerospace engineering experience.

The Mojave Sphinx runs on nitrous oxide and an alcohol-based fuel, with a propulsion system built around a heatsink combustion chamber, scrintle injector, and a custom spark torch igniter. A key milestone during my involvement was achieving stable combustion in a successful hot fire of the spark torch igniter.

PROPULSION

Worked on the scrintle injector and heatsink combustion chamber designed for reliable ignition and stable combustion of nitrous oxide and alcohol fuel. The spark torch igniter — a critical sub-system — required precision in design and careful management of propellant flow, with multiple iterations based on data from previous test attempts.

FLUIDS SYSTEM

Contributed to the propellant feed system, which uses servo-actuated ball valves, stainless steel and PTFE feedlines, and passive dual-acting vapour pressure pressurisation to control the full propellant flow path from tanks to combustion chamber.

TESTING

Supported test stand setup and instrumentation for static fire testing at Minuteman Airfield in Stow, Massachusetts. Data and control systems handled remote ignition commands and logged chamber pressure and valve state throughout each burn.
