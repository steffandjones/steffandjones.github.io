Education Access Initiative

Closing the guidance gap for Welsh students aiming high

Why I Started This

After growing up in Wales and studying Mechanical Engineering at Harvard, I noticed a clear gap: many Welsh students have the ability to compete for top universities, but far fewer have access to the mentorship, application knowledge, and confidence-building support that students in better-resourced areas often take for granted.

I started this work to make that path more visible and more navigable - especially for students targeting Russell Group and highly selective international universities. The goal isn’t “tutoring as a product”; it’s building a practical support system that helps talented students translate potential into outcomes.

What I Built

  • Academic support: Structured tutoring for GCSE, A-Level, and university STEM (plus broader subjects when needed)
  • University guidance: Planning, positioning, essays, and interview preparation - with a focus on clarity and confidence
  • US admissions support: SAT/ACT prep and application strategy for students exploring American universities
  • Bilingual access: Sessions available in both English and Welsh

How I Approach It

I work like an engineer: diagnose the problem, build a plan, iterate quickly, and track progress. For academics, that means identifying where marks are being lost and fixing those patterns. For applications, it means turning a student’s story into a clear, credible narrative - without trying to “manufacture” someone into a different person.

My teaching background includes leading course support at Harvard (Thermodynamics / Heat Transfer) and tutoring across age groups, including students with additional learning needs. The through-line is the same: meet students where they are, then raise the ceiling.

Evidence of Impact

  • Teaching leadership: Lead Course Assistant roles supporting large cohorts with technical content
  • Mentorship: Experience supporting Welsh students through academic and personal transition points
  • Clarity under pressure: Students consistently cite “breaking down hard topics” and confidence-building as outcomes
