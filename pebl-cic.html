PEBL-CIC

Product Design Engineer / Software Engineering

Role Overview

Product Design Engineer and Software Engineering role at PEBL CIC, a social enterprise developing innovative technology solutions for agricultural and environmental monitoring. Based in Swansea, Wales, PEBL creates tools that support farmers and coastal communities through smart sensing technology.

The role focused on firmware and software development in preparation for product launch in 2026, working on the GrowProbe and SubCam systems, embedded devices for environmental data collection and monitoring.

Key Responsibilities

  • Writing and testing firmware for sensors, logging, and communications
  • Developing low-level software for data capture, storage, and transfer
  • Testing and debugging electronics in laboratory and field environments
  • Improving system reliability, power efficiency, and usability
  • Translating field feedback into firmware and software improvements
  • Integrating electronics into product housings and mounts
  • Supporting mechanical design changes for sensors and connectors

Technical Work

Software/App Development: Designed and built the front end of the PEBL app — the primary interface through which farmers and coastal operators view and interact with data from the GrowProbe and SubCam devices. The design prioritised clarity and simplicity for non-technical field users accessing the app outdoors, often on variable data connections. This involved decisions about information architecture, visual hierarchy, and how to surface complex sensor readings in a format that is immediately interpretable and actionable in the field.

Connectivity: Engineered device compatibility with 4G wireless networks, enabling automatic data upload from remotely deployed sensors without requiring physical retrieval or local Wi-Fi. This involved selecting and integrating a cellular communication module, writing connection management code to handle the patchy coverage typical of rural and coastal environments, and ensuring that data logged during periods of no signal was reliably buffered on-device and transmitted in full once connectivity was re-established.

Firmware: Wrote Arduino (.ino) firmware to control a motor-driven sonar scanning system on the SubCam device. The firmware manages a motor to rotate the sonar transducer through a defined angular sweep, triggering a distance reading at each step and logging it alongside the corresponding angle. This allows the SubCam to build up a radial scan of the underwater environment during each deployment. The firmware handles motor control, sonar trigger-and-read timing, data formatting, and writing to local storage — all within the resource constraints of the embedded microcontroller.

Design Engineering: Integrating electronics into field-deployable products required attention to waterproofing, connector selection, cable management, and thermal behaviour within compact housings. Mechanical design changes to sensors and connectors were driven by real deployment feedback — issues like connector ingress, cable strain at entry points, or awkward assembly in the field led to targeted design iterations. This work sat at the boundary of software and mechanical engineering, requiring fluency in both the electronics being packaged and the physical constraints of the products they lived in.
