FSAE
AERODYNAMICS

Aerodynamics Co-Lead — Harvard's first FSAE car, Crimson Motorsport. Full aero package design, CFD simulation, and carbon composite manufacturing.

As Aerodynamics Co-Lead for Harvard's inaugural Formula SAE team (Crimson Motorsport), I led the design and development of the complete aerodynamic package for our first competitive race car. This senior capstone project was the culmination of my mechanical engineering education and my passion for automotive performance.

The primary objective was to maximise downforce while minimising drag, improving cornering speeds and overall handling stability for FSAE circuits — typically tight turns and short straights.

MECHANICAL DESIGN

Designed front and rear wing assemblies and underbody aerodynamics from scratch, within FSAE's strict dimensional rules. The split front wing featured mechanically adjustable angle of attack. The rear wing used a multi-element main-plane-and-flap design with end plates sized to minimise tip vortex losses. Collaborated with the chassis team to integrate diffuser geometry exploiting ground effect.

CFD & SIMULATION

Used SimScale for early-stage iteration — fast turnaround allowing multiple configurations to be compared — and ANSYS Fluent for high-fidelity validation of the final design. Key outputs: lift coefficient, drag coefficient, and centre of pressure. Mesh sensitivity studies verified numerical accuracy and boundary conditions matched the expected FSAE speed range.

COMPOSITES & FABRICATION

Carbon fibre skin over foam cores, built in composite wet rooms. Fabric cut to pattern, wetted, laid over foam cores, vacuum-bagged for cure. 3D printed wing profiles served as geometry references throughout. Multiple prototypes at each stage to verify surface accuracy against CAD.

DOCUMENTATION & LEADERSHIP

Project culminated in a 100+ page technical paper covering background theory, FSAE rule constraints, CFD methodology and sensitivity analysis, material selection, composite manufacturing process, and test and validation results. Full thesis presentation delivered to the engineering faculty.
