Alesi Surgical Internship

Medical Device Development · Ultravision 2

Project Overview

Engineering internship at Alesi Surgical LTD, contributing to the development of the Ultravision 2 medical device system. Gained experience in medical device engineering, regulatory requirements, and product testing in a healthcare technology environment.

Key Achievements

  • Supported medical device product development
  • Conducted testing and validation procedures
  • Assisted with regulatory documentation
  • Collaborated on design improvements
  • Gained insight into medical device regulations

Technical Details

Electrical Engineering: Designed and built custom electronic test rigs from scratch — starting from circuit requirements, through component selection, breadboarding, and final assembly. This included frequency sensor circuits with appropriate signal conditioning to interface with AMETEK validation instrumentation. The rigs were built to measure specific electrical characteristics of the Ultravision 2 ION+ Bipolar device, a surgical energy tool operating at controlled radiofrequency outputs, allowing the engineering team to characterise device performance and identify variation across different operating conditions and production units.

Mechanical Design: Contributed to mechanical design work on device components, using CAD to develop and iterate on parts within the tight constraints of a medical device environment. Medical-grade components must account for material biocompatibility, compatibility with sterilisation cycles, and the practicalities of clinical assembly and disassembly. Design iterations were reviewed against device specifications and manufacturing requirements before any physical prototypes were produced, following the structured design control process of a regulated product development programme.

Testing & R&D: Ran structured test campaigns designed to build the evidence base needed for both design decisions and regulatory submissions. Testing included validation of device performance against synthetic tissue models mimicking the electrical and mechanical properties of patient tissue, assessing whether device outputs fell within intended clinical parameters across the full operating range. All results were captured, processed, and documented in a format consistent with ISO 13485 quality management and traceability requirements, with findings fed directly back into the engineering team's design iteration cycle.
